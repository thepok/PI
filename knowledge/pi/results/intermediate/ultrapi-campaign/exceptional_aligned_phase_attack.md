## Outcome and claim status

No proof that every finite decimal word occurs in pi was obtained.  Canonical
V1 remains a `conjecture`.  In particular, this report does **not** establish
the existence of an aligned exceptional phase.

The preceding restricted-denominator audit proved that if

\[
 d\in\mathfrak D_{10},\qquad d\mid 10^N-16,\qquad
 k={10^N-16\over d},                                             \tag{1}
\]

where \(\mathfrak D_{10}\) is the set of positive integers using only the
decimal digits 0 and 1, then

\[
 k>{(N-\log_{10}32)^{\log_2 10}\over20},\qquad {k\over N^2}\to\infty
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]

along every unbounded aligned family.  Thus Iyer's guaranteed
\(O(N^{-2})\) phase cannot transfer.  The only remaining opening is

\[
                         k\,\|d\pi\|_{\mathbb T}\longrightarrow0. \tag{3}
\]

This report narrows (3) in two rigorous ways, recorded as a `proof sketch`:

1. the alignment exponents for each fixed \(d\) form either the empty set or
   one arithmetic progression, and a fixed \(d\) can never contribute to
   (3); and
2. the best published upper bound for the irrationality measure of pi forces
   every survivor into the explicit size band

   \[
   {d\over(10^N-16)^{125/888}}\longrightarrow\infty,qquad
   {k\over(10^N-16)^{763/888}}\longrightarrow0.                   \tag{4}
   \]

The bounded source search is `literature-checked` as of the audit date.  No
checked primary source supplies fixed-pi cancellation on the aligned divisor
set.  The exact finite replay is an `experiment`: among all 2,047 eligible
0/1 integers with 2 through 12 decimal digits, 532 are aligned for some
exponent, but every first-alignment product in the box satisfies
\(k\|d\pi\|>10^{180}\).  Finite evidence is not a proof of failure or of V1.

## 1. Normalized residual statement and quantifiers

Let

\[
 \mathfrak D_{10}
 =\left\{\sum_{j\geq0}\varepsilon_j10^j:
   \varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\},\quad0<\sum_j\varepsilon_j<\infty\right\}.
\]

The exact residual question is:

> Do there exist integers \(N_j\to\infty\) and
> \(d_j\in\mathfrak D_{10}\) such that \(d_j\mid10^{N_j}-16\) and,
> for \(k_j=(10^{N_j}-16)/d_j\), one has
> \(k_j\|d_j\pi\|_{\mathbb T}\to0\)?

The quantifiers that must not be conflated are:

- \(d_j\) must be selected from the decimal 0/1 language;
- the same \(d_j\) must satisfy the multiplicative congruence
  \(10^{N_j}\equiv16\pmod {d_j}\);
- the same pair must have the exceptional fixed-pi phase in (3); and
- the complementary quotient \(k_j\), not just \(d_j\), weights that phase.

The fixed-return bridge gives

\[
 \|(10^N-16)\pi\|_{\mathbb T}
 =\|k(d\pi)\|_{\mathbb T}
 \leq k\|d\pi\|_{\mathbb T}.                                    \tag{5}
\]

Therefore (3) would imply the fixed return and hence V1.  The converse is not
asserted: (3) is a stronger, factor-compatible way to obtain the return.

## 2. Exact multiplicative structure of the aligned set

Every aligned \(d>1\) ends in 1 and is coprime to 10.  Put

\[
 t_d=\operatorname{ord}_d(10),\qquad
 \mathcal A(d)=\{N\geq0:10^N\equiv16\pmod d\}.
\]

**Alignment-progression lemma (`proof sketch`).**  The set
\(\mathcal A(d)\) is either empty or has the form

\[
                  \mathcal A(d)=N_0(d)+t_d\mathbb Z_{\geq0},      \tag{6}
\]

with the obvious truncation if the least nonnegative representative is used.
Indeed, if \(N_0\) is one solution, then another exponent \(N\) is a solution
exactly when \(10^{N-N_0}\equiv1\pmod d\), which is equivalent to
\(t_d\mid N-N_0\).

The prime-power form makes the sieve exact.  Write
\(d=\prod_r p_r^{a_r}\).  For each prime power, either 16 is absent from the
cyclic subgroup generated by 10, or there is one residue

\[
 N\equiv n_r\pmod {t_r},\qquad
 t_r=\operatorname{ord}_{p_r^{a_r}}(10).                         \tag{7}
\]

The global alignment exists exactly when every local logarithm exists and

\[
                       n_r\equiv n_s\pmod{\gcd(t_r,t_s)}          \tag{8}
\]

for every pair.  This is the generalized Chinese remainder theorem.  Thus the
aligned divisor set is not merely a digit subset-sum family: it is the
intersection of that additive digit language with compatible discrete-log
fibres at every prime power.

There is an immediate global consequence.

**No fixed-denominator survivor (`proof sketch`).**  For fixed \(d>1\) with
nonempty \(\mathcal A(d)\), irrationality of pi gives
\(\delta_d=\|d\pi\|_{\mathbb T}>0\).  Along (6),

\[
 {10^N-16\over d}\,\delta_d\longrightarrow\infty.                \tag{9}
\]

Hence every sequence satisfying (3) has \(d_j\to\infty\); in particular, one
may pass to a subsequence whose denominators are strictly increasing and
therefore distinct.  Reusing a favorable small phase at one aligned
denominator cannot work by moving farther along its exponent progression.

This also locates the additive mismatch with Iyer.  Writing
\(d=\sum\varepsilon_r10^r\) gives

\[
 \|d\pi\|=\left\|\sum_r\varepsilon_r(10^r\pi)\right\|.           \tag{10}
\]

Iyer controls a minimum over all \(2^L\) subset sums of this form.  Conditions
(7)--(8) retain only the subset sums whose *integer values* lie in compatible
discrete-log fibres.  No theorem in that paper preserves its discrepancy
estimate after this nonlinear multiplicative filtering.

## 3. A published irrationality-measure cutoff

Zeilberger and Zudilin prove

\[
                         \mu(\pi)\leq7.103205334137\ldots,         \tag{11}
\]

where \(\mu\) is the irrationality exponent.  This was checked in the
[open arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06345v2) and the published
[Moscow Journal paper](https://doi.org/10.2140/moscow.2020.9.407).
In particular, the safe rational exponent

\[
                   \eta=7.104={888\over125}                       \tag{12}
\]

has the following consequence: for all sufficiently large denominators
\(q\), and every integer \(a\),

\[
                         \left|\pi-{a\over q}\right|>q^{-\eta}.  \tag{13}
\]

**Exceptional-phase size theorem (`proof sketch`).**  Suppose an aligned
sequence satisfies (3), and set \(Q_j=10^{N_j}-16=k_jd_j\).  For a nearest
integer \(a_j\) to \(d_j\pi\), (13) gives, eventually,

\[
 {\|d_j\pi\|\over d_j}
 =\left|\pi-{a_j\over d_j}\right|>d_j^{-888/125}.                \tag{14}
\]

The fraction need not be reduced; reducing it only decreases its denominator
and makes the lower bound at least as strong.  Multiplication by \(k_j\)
therefore yields

\[
 k_j\|d_j\pi\|>{k_j\over d_j^{763/125}}
 ={Q_j\over d_j^{888/125}}.                                     \tag{15}
\]

If the left side tends to zero, so must the right side.  Rearranging gives the
two equivalent limits

\[
 \boxed{
 {d_j\over Q_j^{125/888}}\to\infty,qquad
 {k_j\over Q_j^{763/888}}\to0.}                                 \tag{16}
\]

Since \(Q_j/10^{N_j}\to1\), every exceptional aligned sequence must satisfy

\[
 {d_j\over10^{(125/888)N_j}}\to\infty,qquad
 {k_j\over10^{(763/888)N_j}}\to0,                               \tag{17}
\]

where

\[
 {125\over888}=0.1407657657\ldots,qquad
 {763\over888}=0.8592342342\ldots.                              \tag{18}
\]

Thus the current irrationality measure completely excludes the large-cofactor
region \(k\not=o(d^{763/125})\).  It does not exclude the complementary
region.  This limitation is structural: a scalar irrationality measure only
supplies a lower bound, while (3) asks for upper approximation on a thin,
correlated sequence.

Combining (2), (3), and (16), a survivor must simultaneously obey

\[
 k=\Omega\!\left(N^{\log_2 10}\right),\qquad
 \|d\pi\|=o\!\left(N^{-\log_2 10}\right),
 \qquad k=o\!\left((10^N-16)^{763/888}\right).                   \tag{19}
\]

The phase requirement in (19) is already strictly stronger than Iyer's
published \(O(N^{-2})\) guarantee.  If \(k\) is exponential in \(N\), the
required phase is exponentially small as well.

## 4. Exact finite experiment

The companion checker
[`exceptional_aligned_phase_check.py`](exceptional_aligned_phase_check.py)
enumerates every decimal 0/1 integer with 2 through 12 digits that begins and
ends in 1.  There are

\[
                         \sum_{L=2}^{12}2^{L-2}=2047             \tag{20}
\]

such candidates.  Exact factorization, prime-power discrete logarithms, and
generalized CRT merging give:

| decimal length \(L\) | candidates | aligned |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1 | 0 |
| 3 | 2 | 0 |
| 4 | 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 8 | 4 |
| 6 | 16 | 6 |
| 7 | 32 | 8 |
| 8 | 64 | 19 |
| 9 | 128 | 41 |
| 10 | 256 | 55 |
| 11 | 512 | 156 |
| 12 | 1,024 | 241 |
| **total** | **2,047** | **532** |

The remaining candidates split exactly into 1,277 local subgroup failures and
238 CRT incompatibilities.  Representative calculations are:

- \(d=101\): 16 is not in the subgroup generated by 10 modulo 101;
- \(d=1011=3\cdot337\): the least alignment is \(N_0=208\), with
  \(t_d=336\); and
- \(d=111101=241\cdot461\): the local conditions are
  \(N\equiv25\pmod{30}\) and \(N\equiv216\pmod{460}\), which are
  incompatible modulo \(\gcd(30,460)=10\).

The least first-alignment exponent in the entire box is

\[
                         d=1101,qquad N_0=190.                   \tag{21}
\]

The smallest fixed-pi phase in the box occurs instead at

\[
 d=10010100101,quad N_0=948452584,quad
 \|d\pi\|=0.0000473089040236\ldots.                              \tag{22}
\]

The checker uses a stored 100-decimal-place rational enclosure of pi only for
this bounded phase calculation.  It certifies throughout the box that

\[
 N_0-L-1\geq185,qquad \|d\pi\|>4\cdot10^{-5}.                   \tag{23}
\]

Because \(d<10^L\) and \(10^{N_0}-16>10^{N_0-1}\), (23) gives the uniform
finite certificate

\[
 {10^{N_0}-16\over d}\|d\pi\|>10^{180}.                         \tag{24}
\]

This is an `experiment`, not an asymptotic theorem.  Its useful falsification
is narrower: a small phase at an aligned denominator does not help if its
first discrete-log exponent is enormous.  Equation (22) is the clearest
example—its phase is unusually small in the box, but its cofactor overwhelms
it by hundreds of millions of decimal orders of magnitude.

## 5. Literature applicability audit

The primary-source search used the terms *pi irrationality measure*,
*restricted denominators*, *prescribed denominators*, *lacunary dilates modulo
one*, *missing-digit divisors*, and *digits of divisors of \(10^n-a\)*.

- Zeilberger--Zudilin gives the fixed-pi lower bound used in (13)--(17), but
  it has no digit or discrete-log restriction and cannot construct a small
  phase.
- Iyer's
  [digit-restricted denominator theorem](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haaf007)
  selects from the full high-entropy subset-sum family and has no alignment
  condition.  Its \(N^{-2}\) scale is ruled out by (2).
- Schleischitz's
  [extrinsic Cantor approximation theorem](https://doi.org/10.1017/etds.2020.7)
  supplies (2), hence the necessary phase decay in (19), but it contains no
  information about the fixed value pi.
- General lacunary-dilate results located in the search are metric
  (almost-every dilation) or existence statements for a dilation.  They do
  not specialize their exceptional-set conclusions to pi, and they do not
  impose the aligned divisor filter (7)--(8).
- Sanford's 2026 restricted-denominator theorem requires positive
  Diophantine density of the denominator set.  No such theorem is proved for
  the aligned 0/1 divisors; the bounded classification above shows why this
  cannot be inferred from the density of the full digit language.

No checked theorem controls the signed subset sum (10) after conditioning on
all the prime-power logarithm constraints.  That joint additive--multiplicative
estimate is the exact missing input.

## Bottom line

The exact Iyer survivor has not produced V1.  It is now confined to unbounded
distinct 0/1 denominators satisfying compatible prime-power discrete logs,
phases smaller than \(N^{-\log_2 10}\), and the explicit irrationality-measure
band (16).  Every fixed denominator and the entire large-cofactor regime are
closed.  What remains is precisely an exceptional fixed-pi cancellation on a
thin correlated family; neither the checked literature nor the finite search
proves that it occurs.  V1 remains a `conjecture`.
