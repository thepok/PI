## 1. Topological proposition

Let \(M\) be an infinite forward-minimal compact set for \(T_a\), and put
\(M_t=T_b^tM\).

1. A rational circle point has a finite forward \(T_a\)-orbit.  If it belonged
   to \(M\), forward minimality would make \(M\) that finite orbit closure.
   Thus every point of \(M\) is irrational.
2. The map \(T_b^t:M\to M_t\) is a continuous surjective factor commuting
   with \(T_a\).  A factor of a forward-minimal system is forward-minimal.
   If its image were finite, its preimage under the degree-\(b^t\) circle map
   would be finite and would contain \(M\).  Hence each \(M_t\) is infinite.
3. If two compact forward-minimal \(T_a\)-sets meet at \(x\), the closure of
   the forward orbit of \(x\) equals both, so the sets are equal.
4. If \(M_s=M_t\) for \(s<t\), then
   \(T_b^{t-s}M_s=M_s\).  The semigroup generated by \(a\) and
   \(b^{t-s}\) is non-lacunary: otherwise those two generators would be
   powers of one integer and hence multiplicatively dependent.  Furstenberg
   IV.1 makes the joint orbit of any \(x\in M_s\) dense in the circle.  That
   orbit stays in \(M_s\), so \(M_s=\mathbb T\), contradicting minimality
   because \(\{0\}\) is a proper invariant orbit closure.  Therefore the
   slices are pairwise disjoint.
5. For any irrational \(x\in M\), Furstenberg makes
   \(\{a^u b^t x:u,t\ge0\}\) dense.  Its fixed-\(t\) row lies in \(M_t\),
   proving that the union of the pairwise disjoint slices is dense.

This also verifies the forward-action conventions: no inverse of \(T_a\) or
\(T_b\) is used.

## 2. Dimension and entropy preservation

For every set \(E\subset\mathbb T\), the map \(T_c\) is globally Lipschitz,
so \(\dim_H(T_cE)\le\dim_HE\).  Refine the circle into finitely many subarcs
on which \(T_c\) is injective and its image avoids the circle cut.  The inverse
branch is Lipschitz there, so the reverse inequality holds on each piece.
Hausdorff dimension takes the maximum over a finite union, giving

\[
                    \dim_H(T_cE)=\dim_HE.                 \tag{A1}
\]

For entropy, let \(\phi=T_b^t:M\to M_t\) and \(K=b^t\).  Every fibre of
\(\phi\) has at most \(K\) points.  For any finite measurable partition
\(\mathcal P\), conditioning an \(N\)-name on the complete factor point
leaves at most \(K\) possible lifts, hence

\[
 H_\mu\!\left(\bigvee_{j=0}^{N-1}T_a^{-j}\mathcal P
       \mathrel{\Big|}\phi^{-1}\mathcal B(M_t)\right)\le\log K. \tag{A2}
\]

After division by \(N\), the relative entropy is zero.  Thus every invariant
\(\mu\) and \(\phi_*\mu\) have equal measure entropy.  The factor inequality
and the variational principle in the other direction yield

\[
 h_{\rm top}(M,T_a)=h_{\rm top}(M_t,T_a).                 \tag{A3}
\]

This argument is valid for the noninvertible maps here; the uniformly bounded
fibre, not invertibility, is the relevant fact.

## 3. Explicit Toeplitz realization

The pinned AKL construction with \(a_n=5^n\) gives a one-sided Toeplitz point
\(z_5\in\{-1,0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\).  For each coordinate there is a positive
period, and the least common multiple of the finitely many coordinate periods
in any word is a period for that whole word.  Consequently every word in
\(z_5\) recurs on an arithmetic progression with bounded gaps.  This proves
uniform recurrence and hence minimality of its one-sided orbit closure
\(X_5\).

Downarowicz--Kasjan Section 8 proves for the same construction

\[
 h_{\rm top}(X_5)\ge
 \left({6\over\pi^2}-\rho_*\right)\log2,
 \qquad \rho_*\le\sum_{n\ge1}5^{-n}={1\over4}.             \tag{A4}
\]

Thus the lower bound used in the frozen report is source-supported.  Passing
between the one-sided language and its bilateral natural extension does not
change block complexity or entropy.

After any symbolwise injection into \(\{0,1,2\}\), the decimal coding

\[
 \kappa(d)=\sum_{j\ge0}d_j10^{-j-1}
\]

is injective.  If the first difference is at \(j\), then

\[
 |\kappa(d)-\kappa(e)|
 \ge 10^{-j-1}-2\sum_{k>j}10^{-k-1}
 ={7\over9}10^{-j-1}.                                   \tag{A5}
\]

The corresponding upper bound is \((20/9)10^{-j-1}\), so the coding is
bi-Lipschitz and conjugates the one-sided shift to \(T_{10}\).  The standard
cylinder-cover argument (or an entropy-maximizing ergodic measure plus
Shannon--McMillan--Breiman for the lower bound) gives
\(\dim_HX=h_{\rm top}(X)/\log10\) in this metric.  The resulting circle set
is therefore an explicit infinite minimal set of positive entropy and
positive dimension, and Proposition 2.1 applies exactly as claimed.

## 4. The \(K_9\) coding and pushed measures

For \(d,e\in\{0,\ldots,8\}^{\mathbb N}\) first differing at position
\(j\ge1\),

\[
 |\kappa_9(d)-\kappa_9(e)|
 \ge10^{-j}-8\sum_{k>j}10^{-k}
 ={1\over9}10^{-j}>0.                                   \tag{A6}
\]

Hence there is no endpoint or double-expansion collision inside the stated
alphabet.  The continuous coding is a homeomorphism onto \(K_9\) and obeys
\(\kappa_9\sigma=T_{10}\kappa_9\).  It sends the uniform nine-symbol
Bernoulli measure to a \(T_{10}\)-invariant ergodic measure \(\mu\), with

\[
 h_\mu(T_{10})=\log9,\qquad
 \dim_HK_9={\log9\over\log10}>{19\over20};               \tag{A7}
\]

the last comparison is exactly \(9^{20}>10^{19}\).

Commutation makes \(\mu_t=(T_{16}^t)_*\mu\) invariant and ergodic for
\(T_{10}\).  The factor has at most \(16^t\) points per fibre, so (A2) gives

\[
                         h_{\mu_t}(T_{10})=\log9.          \tag{A8}
\]

The support of \(\mu_t\) is exactly \(T_{16}^tK_9\): continuous images of a
compact full-support measure have image support.  Equation (A1) preserves its
dimension, and every support contains zero.

## 5. Rudolph and Host applicability

Suppose \(s<t\), put \(r=t-s\), and assume \(\mu_s=\mu_t=\nu\).  Pushforward
composition, without cancellation or an inverse, gives

\[
 (T_{16}^r)_*\nu=(T_{16}^{r+s})_*\mu=\mu_t=\nu.           \tag{A9}
\]

The measure \(\nu\) is already ergodic for \(T_{10}\).  A set invariant for
the joint \((T_{10},T_{16}^r)\)-action is in particular \(T_{10}\)-invariant,
so \(\nu\) is joint-action ergodic.  Rudolph Corollary 4.11 takes

\[
 u=2,\quad v=5,\quad
 (n_1,m_1)=(1,1),\quad(n_2,m_2)=(4r,0),\quad
 n_1m_2-m_1n_2=-4r.                                    \tag{A10}
\]

Its conclusion is that a non-Lebesgue jointly invariant ergodic measure has
zero entropy for both \(\times10\) and \(\times16^r\).  Equation (A8) is
positive, so \(\nu\) would have to be Lebesgue.  But Lebesgue \(T_{10}\)
entropy is \(\log10\), contradicting (A8).  Therefore the \(\mu_t\) are
distinct.  Distinct ergodic probabilities for the same map are mutually
singular (apply Birkhoff to one continuous function separating the two
measures), proving the claimed pairwise singularity.

Hochman Theorem 1.1 applies directly because 10 and 16 are multiplicatively
independent and \(\mu\) is invariant, ergodic, and has positive
\(T_{10}\)-entropy.  Thus for \(\mu\)-almost every \(x\), the \(16^t x\)
orbit equidistributes for Lebesgue measure.  For continuous \(f\), bounded
convergence gives

\[
 \int f\,d\left({1\over N}\sum_{t<N}\mu_t\right)
 =\int {1\over N}\sum_{t<N}f(16^tx)\,d\mu(x)
 \longrightarrow\int f\,d\lambda.                       \tag{A11}
\]

This is precisely weak-star convergence of the Cesaro averages.  It says
nothing about equality or nonsingularity of any fixed pushed measure.

Finally, \(K_9\) contains irrational points and is forward \(T_{10}\)-invariant.
The union \(\bigcup_tT_{16}^tK_9\) contains the full joint orbit of any such
point, so Furstenberg IV.1 makes that union dense.  A Bernoulli-generic point
has dense decimal shift orbit in the full nine-symbol shift and therefore
decimal orbit closure \(K_9\); it still omits digit 9.  This validates the
last separator statement without specializing an almost-everywhere theorem
to pi.

## Bottom line

The report is a valid method-closing `proof sketch`: dense joint dilate clouds
can consist of pairwise disjoint fixed slices, and positive-entropy,
high-dimensional pushed measures can be pairwise singular while their Cesaro
mean converges to Lebesgue measure.  These facts block several tempting
quantifier swaps but do not establish anything pointwise about pi.  The fixed
return and V1 remain unproved.
