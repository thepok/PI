                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                      The fine-scale properties of sequences (an α)n∈N mod 1 with (an )n∈N ⊆ N and α irrational
                                                  have been extensively studied in the last decades. In this article, we prove that for (an )n∈N

                                                  arising from the set of rough numbers with explicit roughness parameters and any badly
                                                  approximable α, (an α)n∈N mod 1 has Poissonian correlations of all orders, and consequently,
                                                  Poissonian gaps. This is the first known explicit sequence (an α)n∈N mod 1 with any of these
                                                  properties. Further, we show that the statement is false for Lebesgue almost every α, thereby
                                                  disproving a conjecture of Larcher and Stockinger [Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 2020]. The
                                                  method of proof makes use of an equidistribution result in Zd in Diophantine Bohr sets which
                                                  is established by appealing to a novel method of examining random walks on Ostrowski digit
                                                  expansions.

Analyzing the gap distribution directly is notoriously difficult. While it is known that having
Poissonian gaps does not imply uniform distribution and thus also not Poissonian correlations
of any order [1], the most promising approach to showing Poissonian gaps usually goes by
proving that a sequence has Poissonian correlations of k-th order for all k ≥ 2, which then
implies that a sequence has Poissonian gaps, see [34, Appendix A] for a proof of this implication.

    Finding the gap distribution of a sequence is considered generally challenging: There are
various deep and widely open conjectures in this area that include the Berry–Tabor Conjecture
from theoretical physics (see [7, 42, 53] for more details), and the Rudnick–Sarnak–(Zaharescu)
Conjecture [54, 55] that was repeated by Heath–Brown [23]: Under a Diophantine condition on
α (which is α being badly approximable in [54]), {n2 α}n∈N has Poissonian gaps, and clearly,
a bound on the Diophantine exponent of α is necessary. While there has been partial progress
[23, 38, 55, 62, 64], the conjecture itself remains widely open.
    As to be expected, only a few examples are known where the gap distribution of a sequence
in [0, 1) has been determined: Lutsko and Technau showed Poissonian gaps for {α(log n)A }n∈N , A >
1, α ∈ R [41]. Other sequences where a nontrivial, but non-Poissonian gap distribution was de-
                             √
termined include notably { n}n∈N by Elkies and McMullen [14], and ({logb n})n∈N by Marklof
and Strömbergsson [44].

    Motivated especially by the Rudnick–Sarnak–Zaharescu Conjecture, various sequences of
the form {an α}n∈N , where (an )n∈N is an increasing integer sequence and α is an irrational
number, have been studied. Since finding explicit examples is considered very challenging, this
happened so far only from a metric point of view: Poissonian correlations respectively gaps
have been proven for {an α}n∈N for Lebesgue-almost every α for various integer sequences
(an )n∈N . While Rudnick and Zaharescu [56] showed Poissonian gap distributions for almost
all α whenever (an )n∈N is lacunary, most other results established so far consider only the
most tractable case k = 2, i.e. Poissonian pair correlations: Rudnick and Sarnak proved that
{nd α}n∈N , d ≥ 2 has PPC for almost all α [54]. A more general examination of the metric
theory of {an α}n∈N was given by Aistleitner, Lewko and Larcher [4] who provided a sufficient
criterion on the additive energy of (an )n∈N in order for {an α}n∈N to have almost surely PPC.
In the appendix of the same article, Bourgain provided a necessary condition. For more results
in this direction, we refer to e.g. [3, 8, 9, 35, 51]. Finally, we mention the work of Walker [65]
who showed that the prime rotation {pn α}n∈N satisfies PPC for Lebesgue-almost no α. We
remark that all these statements are of a metrical nature, or make assumptions on α where no
explicit α is known to satisfy the assumed properties. To the best of the author’s knowledge,
prior to this article no explicit sequence of the form {an α}n∈N with (an )n∈N ⊆ N was known
to have Poissonian correlations even for k = 2, let alone Poissonian gaps.

    In this article, the set of irrational numbers α considered is the set of badly approximable
numbers, and thus coincides with the assumption of the Rudnick–Sarnak Conjecture in [54]. An
irrational α is called badly approximable whenever lim inf n→∞ n∥nα∥ > 0 (where ∥.∥ stands
for the distance to the nearest integer) or equivalently, the partial quotients of the continued
fraction expansion of α are uniformly bounded. The set of badly approximable numbers has
Lebesgue measure 0, but Hausdorff     √ √
                                         dimension 1, and contains prominent explicit examples
such as the Golden Ratio Φ = 1+2 5 , 2, or in general, all irrational solutions arising from a
quadratic polynomial with integer coefficients. The assumption of α being badly approximable
will be essential in our arguments and will be shown to be almost necessary.

Remarks on the theorems, primes in short intervals, and related works
We point out that the main novelty of Theorem 1 compared to earlier works on (an α)n∈N is
the explicit construction of the sequence (an α)n∈N . Furthermore, the choice of various func-

   On the other hand, the assumption of r(x) → 0 arises from a classical sieve-theoretic
obstruction: if we knew a uniform version of the Hardy–Littlewood k-tuple Conjecture [19],
that contains e.g. the Twin Prime Conjecture as a special case, the result would also be true
without r(x) → 0. This leads us to the following Conjecture:
Conjecture (Poissonian Prime rotation). Let α be a badly approximable number. Then the
prime rotation sequence (pn α)n∈N mod 1 has Poissonian correlations of all orders, and con-
sequently, has Poissonian gap distribution.
     We remark that the convergence towards the Poissonian gap distribution appears numeri-
cally to be very slow, both for the rough numbers covered by Theorem 1 and in the conjectured
prime case. At computationally feasible finite cutoffs, the expected asymptotic behaviour is
still strongly obscured by effects arising from the Three Gap Theorem, making the gap distri-
bution look distinctly non-Poissonian at first sight. This is largely due to the comparatively
high density of the primes and rough numbers: for instance, for the much sparser sequence
of squares, a Poissonian-looking gap distribution is already visible at substantially smaller
cutoffs. For a more detailed explanation of this finite-scale phenomenon, together with fur-
ther numerical experiments supporting the Poissonian Prime rotation Conjecture, we refer the
reader to the Appendix.

   Note that there is a genuine obstruction for proving the Conjecture by our method with
currently known estimates: We employ asymptotic sieve estimates to differences of rough
numbers. In the case of prime numbers, these estimates could only be established if we knew
a uniform version of the Hardy–Littlewood k-tuple Conjecture [19], that contains e.g. the
asymptotic Twin Prime Conjecture as a special case. In order to circumvent such widely open

conjectures, one would (already for k = 2) need to count the number of prime pairs p, p′ ≤ x
with p − p′ = n on a weighted average over the Bohr set B(α, x) = {n ≤ x : ∥nα∥ ≤ logx x },
see Section 2 for context. In the method employed for Theorem 1, we apply these estimates
point-wise (in n), not making use of the averaging information at this stage of the proof. We
can only establish this point-wise bound because of the roughness assumption f (x) = xo(1)
instead of primes (i.e. f (x) = x1/2 ). Nevertheless, the Hardy–Littlewood k-tuple Conjecture
is true for almost all integers (see [45, 47]), and thus there might be some hope to adapt the
averaging techniques to also work on the corresponding Bohr sets. While Diophantine Bohr
sets have some rigid additive structure (which is exploited very heavily in the proof of Theorem
1 at a later step when averaging over the arising singular series) that might help to establish
results “on average”, the exponential sparsity #B(α, x) ≍ log x of the Bohr sets is way too thin
for even the most modern tools such as [45].

    Poissonian correlations on the real line – related works. We note that so far, the
focus of {an α}n concentrated, even from a metric perspective, on either monomial sequences,
or arbitrary sequences, with the exception being Walker’s article [65] on primes. However,
various sequences of number-theoretic interest have been studied on the real line: The notion
of Poissonian correlations can also be transferred to the real line, by normalizing an increasing
sequence (xn )n∈N ⊆ R at each step N by the xN − x1 ; e.g., one can study the primes, asking
about the distribution of (pn+1 − pn )/ log N for 1 ≤ n ≤ N . Gallagher [16] showed that under
a uniform version of the Hardy–Littlewood Conjecture (which coincides with the version we
would need to assume to prove the Poissonian Prime rotation Conjecture) that the primes
are Poissonian (as objects on the real line, as described above). Notably, Montgomery and
Soundararajan [48] conjectured that on slightly longer ranges (i.e., counting correlations at
scale H ≥ (log N )1+δ ), the primes are expected to converge to a Gaussian distribution, with
variance H log(N/H) – see also recent advancements e.g. [32, 37] on related matters. Simi-
larly, Freiberg, Kurlberg and Rosenzweig [15] showed Poissonian behaviour (on the real line)
for sums of two squares, subject to an analogous Hardy–Littlewood-type Conjecture on the
sums of two squares, mirroring the result of Gallagher [16].

    We remark that the behaviour of Poissonian correlations on the real line is somewhat
different to studying sequences in [0, 1]: First of all, the simple observation that the sequence
is increasing implies that previous existing gaps are not split, but only marginally rescaled,
while in the case of a fixed sequence in [0, 1), an earlier gap might be split at any time. Clearly,
the re-normalizing factor allows these sequences to be interpreted as triangular arrays in [0, 1),
a more general setup in which further results of number-theoretic interest are known, such as
the works of Hooley [24, 25, 26] on Z∗q who showed Poissonian behaviour for q/φ(q) → ∞, as
well as the works of Kurlberg and Rudnick [34, 33] on quadratic residues for highly-composite
q.

We will make use of the very basic theory of Markov chains in order to obtain the equidistri-

Definition 2. Given a probability space (Ω, A, P), a (time-homogeneous) Markov chain with
finite state space X is a sequence of random variables (Zi )i∈N0 with Zi : Ω → X that satisfies
the following properties:
 (i) For all x0 , . . . , xn+1 ∈ X, n ∈ N0 such that P[Zn = xn , . . . , Z0 = x0 ] > 0, one has

                  P[Zn+1 = xn+1 | Zn = xn , . . . , Z0 = x0 ] = P[Zn+1 = xn+1 | Zn = xn ].

(ii) For all x, y ∈ X and all n, m ∈ N0 such that P[Zn = x] > 0, P[Zm = x] > 0, we have

                             P[Zn+1 = y | Zn = x] = P[Zm+1 = y | Zm = x].

We define the transition matrix P by P (x, y) := P[Zn+1 = y | Zn = x] and write P (k) (x, y) =
P[Zn+k = y | Zn = x]. The initial distribution of (Zi )i∈N0 is the distribution ν on X defined
by ν(x) := P[Z0 = x], x ∈ X.

    We call a Markov chain irreducible when for all x, y ∈ X, there exists n ∈ N such that
P (n) (x, y) > 0. We call a Markov chain aperiodic if for all x ∈ X

                                  gcd({n > 0 : P n (x, x) > 0}) = 1.

We call a distribution π on X stationary if (interpreted as vector πx := π(x), x ∈ X) we
have πP = π.
