                                                    Abstract. Let p be a prime, and N be a positive integer not divisible by p. Denote
                                                    by ordN (p) the multiplicative order of p modulo N . Let Fq represent the finite field
                                                    of order q = pordN (p) . For a, b ∈ Fq , we define a binomial exponential sum by
                                                                                              X           q−1
                                                                              SN (a, b) :=           χ(ax N + bx),
                                                                                         x∈Fq \{0}

                                                    where χ is the canonical additive character of Fq . In this paper, we provide an explicit
                                                    evaluation of SN (a, b) for any odd prime p and any N satisfying ordN (p) = φ(N ).
                                                    Our elementary and direct approach allows for the construction of a class of ternary
                                                    linear codes, with their exact weight distribution determined. Furthermore, we prove
                                                    that the dual codes achieve optimality with respect to the sphere packing bound,
                                                    thereby generalizing previous results from even to odd characteristic fields.

with χ = χ1 . Providing an explicit evaluation of SN (a, b) is of significant interest. In
2009, Moisio [16] gave the explicit evaluation of SN (a, b) for p = 2 and N = ℘m , where
℘ is an odd prime and m is a positive integer such that 2 is a primitive root modulo ℘m .
In 2019, under certain conditions, Wu, Yue and Li [22] generalized the evaluation result
of Moisio to odd characteristics. In fact, they presented an evaluation of S℘m (a, b) for
p ≥ 3 under a condition that the least subfield Fpt of Fq subject to certain restrictions
is Fp or Fp2 , where ℘ is an odd prime and m is a positive integer such that p is a
primitive root modulo ℘m . In this paper, inspired by the work [16], we obtain an explicit
evaluation of SN (a, b) for any positive integer N and any odd prime p, assuming that p is
a primitive root modulo N . More precisely, we shall compute SN (a, b) for N = 2, 4, ℘m
and 2℘m respectively, which are the all possible values of N since there exists a primitive
root modulo N if and only if N = 2, 4, ℘m or 2℘m , where ℘ is an odd prime and m
is a positive integer. Our results are not based on any conditions, and our method is
elementary and straightforward, differing significantly from that used in [22].
   The second objective of the paper is to construct a class of linear codes. Let n, k and
d be positive integers. An [n, k, d] p-ary linear code is a k-dimensional subspace of Fp
with minimum (Hamming) distance d. For a positive integer i, denote by Ai the number
of codewords with Hamming weight i in a code C of length n. Let
                               1 + A1 z + A2 z 2 + · · · + An z n
be the weight enumerate of C. The sequence (A1 , A2 , . . . , An ) is referred to as the weight
distribution of C. If the number of nonzero Ai in the sequence (A1 , A2 , . . . , An ) is N ,
then the code C is called an N -weight code. The weight distribution directly determines
the minimum distance of the code and the error correcting capability. It also contains
key information on computation of the probability of error detection and correction with
respect to some error detection and correction algorithms [13]. As a result, studying the
weight distribution of a linear code is an important research topic in coding theory. For
a set D = {d1 , d2 , . . . , dn } ⊆ F∗q , define a set by
                    CD := {(Tr(d1 x), Tr(d2 x), . . . , Tr(dn x)) : x ∈ Fq }.            (1.1)
Clearly, CD is a linear codes of length over Fp , and we call D the defining set of the
code CD . This defining-set construction is fundamental since every linear code over Fp
can be expressed as CD for some defining set D (possibly multiset) [9]. Many classes of
codes with few weights were produced by properly selecting the defining set D ⊆ Fq (see,
for example, [8, 10, 18, 21]). In the paper, a class of two-weight codes is constructed
motivated by the work of Wang, Ding and Xue [21], and the weight distribution is
determined. The dual codes is also studied, and it is proved to be optimal codes. The
linear codes with two weights presented in this paper have applications in secret sharing
[1], authentication codes [11], combinatorial designs [7], etc.
    Throughout this paper, we always assume that p is an odd prime, N is a positive
integer not divisible by p such that ordN (p) = φ(N ), and q = pφ(N ) . The paper is
organized as follows. First in Section 2, we evaluate SN (a, 0). Subsequently in Section 3,
the explicit evaluation of the exponential sum SN (a, b) with ab 6= 0 is obtained. In Section
4, a class of ternary codes is constructed, and its weight distribution is determined.

                                         6. Conclusion
   In this paper, we provided explicit evaluations of binomial Weil sums SN (a, b) for
all various values of N , assuming that p is a primitive root modulo N . Our results
significantly extend previous work by removing additional conditions and offering a more
general and elementary approach. Moreover, we constructed a new class of ternary linear
codes, for which the weight distribution was fully determined. This weight distribution
analysis demonstrated the optimality of the dual codes with respect to the sphere packing
bound.
   In addition, the method presented in this paper is expected to enable the construction
of general p-ary codes from the evaluations of the exponential sums, and to facilitate the
determination of their Hamming weight distributions. Specifically, let N ∈ {4, ℘m , 2℘m },
and let p be a general odd prime such that p is a primitive root modulo N . By replacing
the defining set
                                                 q−1
                                 {x ∈ F∗q : Tr(x ℘m ) = 0}
with the more general set
                                              q−1       
                           D = {x ∈ F∗q : Tr x N + βx = α},
where α ∈ Fp and β ∈ Fq , similar results for the code CD can be derived using analogous
techniques. Recently, leveraging the results of this paper, the first author [6] established
the weight distribution of CD for any α ∈ Fp and β ∈ Fq when p = 3 and N = 2℘m .
