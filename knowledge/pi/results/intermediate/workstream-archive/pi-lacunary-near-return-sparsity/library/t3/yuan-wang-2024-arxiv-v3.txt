                                                                               1. Introduction

                                             Let (X, d) be a metric space, and let T : X → X be a transformation. If µ is
                                           a T -invariant Borel probability measure on X and T is ergodic with respect to the
                                           measure µ, Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem implies that for any ball B ⊂ X of positive
                                           measure, the subset S = {x ∈ X : T n x ∈ B for i.m. n ∈ N} has full µ-measure.
                                           Here and throughout the paper, ‘i.m.’ stands for ‘infinitely many’. This means that
                                           the trajectories of almost all points will enter the ball B infinitely often. In general,
                                           one may wonder what will happen if B shrinks with time. Let ψ be a function on N
                                           and z ∈ X, the investigation of the size in terms of measure and dimension of the set

                                                           S(ψ, z) = {x ∈ X : T n x ∈ B(z, ψ(n)) for i.m. n ∈ N},

                                           is called shrinking target problems by Hill and Velani [13], where B(z, ψ(n)) ⊂ X
                                           is a ball of center z and radius ψ(n). On the other hand, motivated by Poincaré’s

   The measures and dimensions of the fractal sets S(ψ, z) and R(ψ) have already
been extensively investigated in many dynamical systems. For the measure aspect,
the reader is referred to [1, 2, 9, 10, 12, 16, 17, 19–21] and references therein. With
regards to the dimension aspect, let us cite some but far from all concrete cases.
The dimension aspect has been studied for expanding rational maps of Julia sets [13,
14], matrix transformations of tori [15, 21], β-transformations [18, 27, 29], conformal
iterated function systems [22, 24, 26]. For the more results, the reader is referred to
[3, 4, 23, 33] and references therein.
  It should be observed that in many systems, the fractal dimensional formulae for
the two sets S(ψ) and R(ψ) are the same. In [28, 31, 32, 34], the dimensions of S(ψ)
and R(ψ) were unified in some dynamical systems by using a Lipschitz function. We
are interested in finding out if they can be unified when T is a matrix transformation
on the torus Td .
