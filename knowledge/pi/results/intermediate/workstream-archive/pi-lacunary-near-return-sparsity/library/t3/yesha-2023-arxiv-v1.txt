                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                            A real-valued sequence (xn )∞n=1 is said to be uniformly distributed (or equidistributed)
                                          modulo one, if for every interval I ⊆ [0, 1), we have
                                                                              1
                                                                        lim       # {1 ≤ n ≤ N : {xn } ∈ I} = |I| ,
                                                                      N →∞ N

                                          where {x} denotes the fractional part of x, and |I| denotes the length of the interval I.
                                          There are many examples of sequences which satisfy this property, e.g., the Kronecker
                                          sequence xn = αn where α is irrational, and more generally (as was shown by Weyl in his
                                          pioneering 1916 paper [12]) the sequence xn = αd nd + · · · + α1 n + α0 (αi ∈ R), where at
                                          least one of the coefficients α1 , . . . , αd is irrational. In the metric sense, more can be said:
                                          Weyl proved [12] that for any sequence (an )∞       n=1 of distinct integers, the sequence xn = αan
                                          is uniformly distributed modulo one for (Lebesgue) almost all α ∈ R. This is also true
                                          for real-valued sequences whose elements are sufficiently separated from each other (see,
                                          e.g., [5, Chapter 1, Corollary 4.1]): if (an )∞     n=1 is a real-valued sequence, and there exists
                                          a positive constant δ > 0 such that |an − am | ≥ δ for each n 6= m, then the sequence
                                          xn = αan is uniformly distributed modulo one for almost all α ∈ R. This condition clearly
                                          holds for real-valued, positive, lacunary sequences, i.e., sequences such that a1 > 0, and
                                          there exists a constant C > 1 such that for all n ≥ 1 we have
                                                                                        an+1 ≥ Can .

   While the classical theory deals with the distribution of sequences modulo one at “large”
scales, there has been a growing interest in recent years in the fluctuations of sequences at
smaller scales. For many sequences, it is conjectured (backed up by numerical evidence)
that the small-scale statistics (at the scale 1/N – the mean gap of the first N elements of the
sequence modulo one) are in agreement with the statistics of uniform i.i.d. random points
in the unit interval (Poissonian statistics), thus demonstrating pseudo-random behaviour
for such sequences. A popular small-scale statistic is the (normalized) gap distribution of
the re-ordered first N elements of the sequence modulo one, which for many sequences is
expected to converge to the exponential distribution (“Poissonian gap statistics”) – the
almost sure limiting distribution of the gaps in the random model. Lacunary sequences are
among the rare examples where such behaviour has been rigorously proved to hold (in the
metric sense): Rudnick and Zaharescu proved [10] Poissonian gap statistics for almost all
α ∈ R for the sequence xn = αan where (an )∞      n=1 is an integer-valued lacunary sequence;
this was recently extended to real-valued lacunary sequences by Chaubey and the author
[2].
   Statistics in the “mesoscopic” regime, i.e., at the scale L/N , where L = L (N ) → ∞ and
L = o (N ) as N → ∞, provide further information which may capture some interesting
features of sequences. An example of such a statistic is the number variance (the variance
of the number of elements in random intervals, see the definition below in our setting),
famously studied for the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, for which at small scales the
number variance is consistent with that of the eigenvalues of random matrices drawn from
the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE), whereas “saturation” occurs at larger scales (see
[1]). In the context of sequences modulo one, only a few results have been established
so far in the mesoscopic regime, mainly concerning the leading order asymptotics of the
long-range correlations of the sequence xn = αn2 (see [11, 6, 4]); nevertheless, important
intermediate-scale statistics such as the number variance have largely remained unexplored.
The goal of this paper is to study such statistics for real-valued lacunary sequences.
