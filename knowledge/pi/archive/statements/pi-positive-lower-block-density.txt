CANONICAL QUESTION
Let pi = 3.d_1 d_2 d_3 ... be its unique nonterminating decimal expansion, with D = {0,...,9} and d_i in D for every integer i >= 1. For integers k,N >= 1 and w = (w_0,...,w_(k-1)) in D^k, define A_pi(w,N) to be the number of integers n with 0 <= n < N such that d_(n+j+1) = w_j for every integer j with 0 <= j < k. Is it true that, for every k >= 1 and every w in D^k, liminf_(N->infinity) A_pi(w,N)/N > 0? Status: OPEN pending a source-pinned literature audit. This is stronger than local:pi-digits but weaker than base-10 normality.

RECORDED AMBIGUITIES
A1 (CANONICAL): every nonempty decimal word, including leading-zero words, has strictly positive lower asymptotic frequency as quantified above. Status: OPEN.
A2: every word occurs at least once. This is local:pi-digits, is weaker than A1, and is OPEN.
A3: every word occurs infinitely often or arbitrarily late. T21 proves this equivalent to A2 for a generic one-sided stream; it is not A1.
A4: every word has positive upper asymptotic frequency. This is weaker than A1 because favorable subsequences may differ by word. Status: OPEN for pi.
A5: every word has an existing positive limiting frequency, without prescribing its value. This is stronger than A1 but weaker than normality. Status: OPEN.
A6: every length-k word has limiting frequency 10^(-k). This is base-10 normality, is stronger than A1, and is OPEN for pi.
A7: every word occurs with bounded gaps, or has positive lower Banach density. These are stronger recurrence readings and are not canonical. Status: OPEN.
A8: every weak-* accumulation point of mu_N = N^(-1) sum_(n=0)^(N-1) delta_{fract(10^n*pi)} on R/Z has full topological support. This is the intended dynamical equivalent of A1; the equivalence, including decimal-cylinder boundaries, is an initial formal target rather than an assumed theorem.
A9: at least one empirical accumulation point has full support. This is weaker than A8 but still implies A2; it is not canonical. Status: OPEN.
A10: for each k there is an unspecified delta_k > 0 uniform over all w in D^k. Because D^k is finite, this is equivalent to A1. Requiring an explicit bound such as delta_k >= c*10^(-k) is stronger and not canonical.
A11: bound only the first occurrence by C*k*10^k. This is the separate pi-quantitative-block-hitting problem; it neither supplies repeated positive density nor follows with that rate from A1.
A12: count noncontiguous subsequences, prefixes only, aligned starts, or include the integer digit 3. These conventions are excluded. Starting empirical averages at n=1 instead of n=0 is an equivalent finite shift.
A13: replace base 10 by base 2, base 16, or all bases. These are different problems; cross-base transfer cannot be assumed.
A14: use logarithmic, Abel, or other weighted averages. These define different density notions and are excluded.
