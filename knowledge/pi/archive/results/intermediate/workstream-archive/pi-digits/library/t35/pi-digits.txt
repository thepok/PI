PROBLEM local:pi-digits — canonical statement (2026-07-21, human-authored root)

CANONICAL QUESTION
Does every finite sequence of decimal digits occur as a contiguous block
somewhere in the decimal expansion of pi?

Equivalently: is pi disjunctive (rich) in base 10? This is implied by (and
weaker than) the conjecture that pi is normal in base 10. Status: OPEN.
Widely believed true; no known proof technique. It is not even proven that
every single digit occurs infinitely often in the decimal expansion of pi.

PROVENANCE
Root created from Marcel's request of 2026-07-21: "does any integer sequence
occur at some point in the extension of PI?" — normalized to the canonical
question above (an "integer sequence" occurring "in the expansion" is read as
a finite digit string occurring contiguously; leading zeros are permitted in
the string, so this covers digit strings rather than integer decimal
representations, which is the stronger and more natural normalization; an
integer's decimal representation is a special case).

RECORDED AMBIGUITIES OF THE ORIGINAL PHRASING
The phrase "any integer sequence" admits three readings. They are distinct
problems and are recorded here so no later work silently swaps targets:

  V1 (CANONICAL, above): every FINITE digit string occurs contiguously.
     OPEN. This root.
  V2: every INFINITE digit sequence occurs contiguously. FALSE, provably:
     a contiguous infinite occurrence is a tail (suffix) of the expansion;
     the expansion has only countably many tails, but there are uncountably
     many infinite digit sequences. (Also elementarily: any two distinct
     tails of occurrence positions force eventual periodicity — but the
     cardinality argument alone suffices.) V2 is a sibling variant suitable
     for complete machine-checked disproof; resolving V2 does NOT resolve V1.
  V3: every infinite digit sequence occurs as a (not necessarily contiguous)
     subsequence. Equivalent to: every digit 0..9 occurs infinitely often in
     the expansion. OPEN (this equivalence is elementary and a good early
     formalization target; the digit statement itself is open).

KNOWN CONTEXT (to be source-pinned by literature work, not trusted from this
file): decimal digits of pi are known to be non-repeating (pi irrational,
Lambert 1761); pi is transcendental (Lindemann 1882); normality of pi is open
in every base; BBP-type formulas give base-16 digit extraction; the
Bailey–Crandall research program reduces normality-type statements to a
conjecture about a specific dynamical iteration; Champernowne's constant is
provably normal in base 10 (a template for what a resolution artifact can
look like for artificial constants).
