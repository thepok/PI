## 1. Exact target relation and quantifiers

For an aligned pair, put

\[
 d\in\mathfrak D_{10},\qquad d\mid 10^N-16,\qquad
 k={10^N-16\over d}>0.                                           \tag{A1}
\]

The circle metric satisfies

\[
 \|(10^N-16)\pi\|_{\mathbb T}
 =\|k(d\pi)\|_{\mathbb T}
 \le k\|d\pi\|_{\mathbb T}.                                   \tag{A2}
\]

Thus a sequence (N_j\to\infty) satisfying

\[
             k_j\|d_j\pi\|_{\mathbb T}\longrightarrow0        \tag{A3}
\]

forces (10^{N_j}\pi\to16\pi\pmod1).  The previously audited T69
bridge, together with Furstenberg's nonlacunary-semigroup theorem, makes this
fixed return sufficient for V1.  The implication is one-way for this divisor
route: V1 need not furnish factors (d_j\in\mathfrak D_{10}) satisfying
(A1)--(A3).  The primary report preserves that distinction.

The four coupled quantifiers are also correct: the same (d_j) must be a
decimal 0/1 integer, divide the same (10^{N_j}-16), have the exceptional
fixed-pi phase, and be weighted by its actual complementary quotient.  A
small phase selected independently of the alignment is irrelevant.

## 2. Alignment progression and CRT classification

If (d>1) divides (10^N-16), then a decimal 0/1 integer (d) cannot end in
zero, because (5\nmid10^N-16).  Hence it ends in one and is coprime to ten.
Let (t_d=\operatorname{ord}_d(10)).  If (N_0) is one solution of
(10^N\equiv16\pmod d), then cancellation by the unit (10^{N_0}) gives

\[
 10^N\equiv16\pmod d
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N\equiv N_0\pmod {t_d}.                                       \tag{A4}
\]

Choosing (0\le N_0<t_d) gives exactly

\[
 \mathcal A(d)=N_0+t_d\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.                         \tag{A5}
\]

For (d=\prod p_r^{a_r}), the cyclic subgroup generated by ten has exactly
one exponent class modulo
(t_r=\operatorname{ord}_{p_r^{a_r}}(10)) mapping to 16, when such a class
exists.  The generalized CRT joins those local classes exactly when

\[
 n_r\equiv n_s\pmod{\gcd(t_r,t_s)}                              \tag{A6}
\]

for every pair.  The global period is
(\operatorname{lcm}_r t_r=\operatorname{ord}_d(10)).  This rederives all
three classifications in the report; no primality or pairwise-coprime-period
assumption is being smuggled into the CRT step.

For fixed (d), irrationality of pi makes
(\delta_d=\|d\pi\|_{\mathbb T}>0), whereas along (A5)
((10^N-16)/d\to\infty).  Therefore a survivor cannot contain any fixed
denominator infinitely often.  If (d_j\not\to\infty), some bounded finite
set would occur along an infinite subsequence, and then one member of that
set would occur infinitely often.  This proves the corrected conclusion
(d_j\to\infty), and only after taking a subsequence may the denominators be
assumed strictly increasing.

The representative residues independently replay as follows:

- (101): (16\notin\langle10\rangle\pmod {101});
- (1011=3\cdot337): (N\equiv0\pmod1) and
  (N\equiv208\pmod {336}), hence (N_0=208) and (t_d=336);
- (111101=241\cdot461): (N\equiv25\pmod {30}) and
  (N\equiv216\pmod {460}), incompatible modulo ten.

## 3. Irrationality-measure cutoff

Zeilberger--Zudilin define the irrationality measure so that, for every
exponent strictly above it, all sufficiently large integer denominators
satisfy the corresponding strict rational-separation bound.  Their final
published value is

\[
 \mu(\pi)\le7.10320533413700172750577342281\ldots<
 {888\over125}=7.104.                                           \tag{A7}
\]

Consequently, for all sufficiently large positive (q) and every integer
(a),

\[
                 \left|\pi-{a\over q}\right|>q^{-888/125}.      \tag{A8}
\]

The source definition itself quantifies over integers (a,q), so no
coprimality premise is needed.  Reduction of a displayed fraction therefore
causes no onset problem in this application.

Choose a nearest integer (a_j) to (d_j\pi).  Since the corrected fixed-
denominator argument gives (d_j\to\infty), (A8) applies eventually and

\[
 {\|d_j\pi\|\over d_j}>d_j^{-888/125},\qquad
 k_j\|d_j\pi\|>{k_j\over d_j^{763/125}}
 ={Q_j\over d_j^{888/125}},                                    \tag{A9}
\]

where (Q_j=k_jd_j=10^{N_j}-16).  If (A3) holds, the final positive term in
(A9) tends to zero.  Since

\[
 {Q\over d^{888/125}}
 =\left({Q^{125/888}\over d}\right)^{888/125},                 \tag{A10}
\]

we obtain

\[
 {d_j\over Q_j^{125/888}}\to\infty.                            \tag{A11}
\]

The second displayed limit in the report is exactly its reciprocal form:

\[
 {k_j\over Q_j^{763/888}}
 ={Q_j^{125/888}\over d_j}\to0.                               \tag{A12}
\]

Thus (125/888) and (763/888) are not heuristic fitted exponents: they are
respectively (1/\eta) and ((\eta-1)/\eta) for
(\eta=888/125).  Since (Q_j/10^{N_j}\to1), replacing (Q_j) by
(10^{N_j}) in these normalized limits is valid.

The wording “large-cofactor region” has the exact meaning

\[
 k_j\not=o(d_j^{763/125})
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{(A3) is impossible}.           \tag{A13}
\]

It does not exclude the complementary small-cofactor region and does not
construct a phase there.

## 4. Inherited cofactor exponent and phase scale

I also rechecked the inherited Schleischitz exponent instead of treating it
as a black-box numerical value.  For the decimal missing-digit set
(C_{01}), put

\[
 \Delta={\log2\over\log10},\qquad {1\over\Delta}=\log_2 10.
\]

Theorem 3.4, equation (11), applied to (1/k\notin C_{01}), together with

\[
 {1\over k}={d\over10^N}+{16\over k10^N},\qquad
 {d\over10^N}\in C_{01},                                      \tag{A14}
\]

gives

\[
 {16\over k10^N}>
 {10^{-20^\Delta k^\Delta}\over2k}.                            \tag{A15}
\]

Cancellation, base-ten logarithms, and raising to (1/\Delta) yield

\[
 k>{(N-\log_{10}32)^{\log_2 10}\over20}.                       \tag{A16}
\]

Because (log_2 10>3>2), this gives (k/N^2\to\infty) along every
unbounded aligned family.  Combining (A3) with (A16) gives

\[
 \|d_j\pi\|=o(1/k_j)=o(N_j^{-\log_2 10}),                      \tag{A17}
\]

which is indeed strictly smaller than Iyer's guaranteed (O(N^{-2}))
scale.  The separate finite-depth inequality (k>N^2) for every (N\ge5)
comes from the elementary/piecewise argument in the preceding audited
report; it is not incorrectly inferred from (A16) at small (N).

## 6. Primary-source applicability

The following source claims and limitations were checked directly on
2026-08-12 UTC:

- Zeilberger--Zudilin, arXiv:1912.06345v2 and the Moscow Journal paper:
  the definition is on printed page 407 and the value in (A7) on printed
  page 418.  Fresh source/PDF hashes agree with all three pins in the primary
  report.  This is a scalar lower bound only; it cannot create a small phase
  on the aligned divisor set.
- Iyer, arXiv:2312.01076v1, Theorem 1.1: the minimum is over the full decimal
  0/1 denominator family and is (O((\log X)^{-2})), uniformly in the real
  input.  The theorem contains no divisibility, multiplicative-order,
  discrete-log, annular-size, or cofactor clause.
- Schleischitz, arXiv:1812.10689v4, Theorem 3.4 equation (11): the explicit
  missing-digit distance bound applies to every outside rational and has
  exactly the exponent used in (A15)--(A16).
- Sanford, arXiv:2606.02620v1, Definition 1 and Theorem 3: the theorem
  assumes positive Diophantine density.  Neither that source nor this report
  proves such density for the multiplicatively filtered aligned divisor set.
- Furstenberg 1967, Theorem IV.1, printed page 48: a nonlacunary integer
  semigroup has dense orbit at every irrational point.  It validates the
  fixed-return-to-V1 bridge but supplies no fixed return itself.

The report's negative literature conclusion is accepted only in its stated
dated, bounded sense: none of the checked primary sources controls the
additive subset sum after imposing every local discrete-log condition.  It
is not an exhaustive theorem that no such result can exist.
